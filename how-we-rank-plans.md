# How we rank plans

Your doctors, prescriptions, expected care, and benefit needs can change which Medicare Advantage plans rise to the top. Our comparison works through a sequence of decisions. A lower premium alone does not determine the recommendation.

## Start with the plans available for your comparison

We use your location, the plan year being compared, the coverage path you selected, and relevant eligibility answers to assemble a candidate list. Plan-type filters, data availability, duplicate removal, and any required doctor-network filter can narrow that list. Some special-needs plans require additional eligibility information. The tool's screening is not an enrollment eligibility determination.

The comparison is limited to the candidates available through this process. It is not a ranking of every Medicare coverage option nationwide, and this methodology does not compare Medicare Advantage against Original Medicare or Medigap.

## Consider your doctors and prescriptions

We give priority to plans with more carrier-confirmed matches for the doctors you entered. We then consider the combined doctor and prescription matches, with additional doctor and prescription comparisons when counts tie.

Doctor evidence has different levels of confidence. A likely network match or an acknowledged match to another network can contribute to the broader match count without becoming a carrier-confirmed match. A separate indication that another network can be checked may help distinguish otherwise equal coverage results; it does not establish coverage by itself.

Unknown network or drug information is not proof that a plan does not cover your care. It also does not earn the same ranking credit as an established match. If you enter no doctors or prescriptions, those factors cannot personalize the result.

## Compare costs and the benefits you expect to use

When coverage priorities are equal, we compare an internal measure that combines:

- Plan premiums and estimated covered prescription costs, less an applicable Part B premium reduction.
- Estimated costs for selected types of medical visits, using your reported level of care and the plan's available cost-sharing information.
- A risk adjustment related to the plan's medical out-of-pocket limit.
- Estimated spending on stated dental, vision, hearing, or over-the-counter needs that the plan does not meet.
- Estimated cash costs for prescriptions outside the plan's formulary, when those costs can be estimated.

The risk adjustment helps distinguish exposure to a costly year. It is not a bill or an estimate of money you will necessarily spend. The resulting internal ranking measure is not your total annual Medicare budget: it does not add your standard Part B premium or model every possible medical service.

A benefit allowance can reduce the unmet spending assigned to that benefit. Unused allowance does not become cash that offsets unrelated costs. Where one allowance is shared across benefits, we limit its combined contribution to avoid counting the same allowance more than once.

Simply asking for a benefit to be available does not automatically create an assumed spending amount. Some follow-up answers use default assumptions; the technical reference explains the distinction.

## Handle uncertainty explicitly

If a necessary medical or prescription cost cannot be estimated, the plan does not receive a zero-dollar estimate. It can still appear based on its coverage matches, but it sorts behind plans with usable cost calculations within the same coverage comparison.

Benefit uncertainty is handled separately. For a stated need, unverified benefit coverage may receive no credit, leaving the full modeled need in the calculation. Some benefit structures instead use an explicit fallback assumption. That is a modeling choice, not confirmation that the benefit is absent.

## Distinguish list order from the highlighted recommendation

The plan highlighted as a recommendation can differ from the first plan in the ordered list. Additional checks include cost-data completeness, quality indicators, and—for someone requesting vision coverage—confirmed absence of routine eye-exam coverage.

These checks do not remove the affected plans from the ranked list. Certain checks relax when no candidates remain eligible under them. Missing information is treated differently from a confirmed negative finding.

If you provide a current plan, we compare it using the same calculation structure. Confirmed coverage gaps can support a change even without a large cost difference. If your current plan covers all the care you entered and every candidate has known coverage that falls short, the comparison can favor staying. Otherwise, a modeled improvement must meet a materiality threshold before the engine recommends switching on cost alone. Insufficient information can prevent a cost-based conclusion.

## Check the details before choosing

Your actual spending depends on the care you receive, how you use benefits, and the plan's terms. Network information, prescription coverage, and costs can change. Verify your doctors, medications, restrictions, and expected services with the plan.

For another way to review plan costs and coverage, use [Medicare's comparison tools](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/using-medicare/helpful-tools).
